[Read v1]({{ '/2025/07/16/webs-dead-baby.html' | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ '/v1.5/2025-07-16-webs-dead-baby/' | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ '/v2/2025-07-16-webs-dead-baby/' | relative_url }})

V1 has a strong emotional subject: the loss of pages that looked unmistakably like their makers. Its comparison of early website building to punk rock gives that loss a social texture. The original song reinforces the sense of an amateur medium becoming professional and then automated.

But the essay’s history is too tidy. Chaotic youth leads to commercial sterility; AI completes the process; creative expression then conveniently migrates somewhere else. The web’s variety becomes a single lifecycle. Contra compounds the problem by treating clean appearance as evidence of accessibility and by asserting a decline in direct visits without support. The synthesis sounds generous, but it reconciles claims the essay has not established.

V1.5 retains the personal-web nostalgia and song while narrowing those claims. The remembered web is identified as one experience rather than a universal golden age. Templates and AI can support experimentation as well as sameness. Accessibility is connected to actual design requirements through W3C guidance, rather than equated with a polished aesthetic. The discussion of intermediaries becomes a possible loss of context, not a forecast that websites no longer matter. Most usefully, the conclusion distinguishes usable conventions from generic content and imagery.

These changes make v1.5 a more trustworthy cultural reflection. They also weaken its provocative title, which now operates mainly as the prompt’s challenge. That is preferable to manufacturing evidence for the obituary. A remaining limitation is that the recommendations address design craft and usability but do not supply the requested deeper reading on commercialization of art.

V2 begins with two visually identical pages: a particular repair account and generic promotional copy. That example immediately separates personal authorship from decorative novelty. The handle-and-room metaphor then gives the dialogue a durable structure: familiar controls can lead into a distinctive arrangement of material. Contra’s argument for layered information prevents the defense of expression from becoming a demand that every visitor admire the author’s sensibility before receiving an answer.

The rewrite also expands “personal” to include control over archives, corrections, links, and portability. That is a substantial change of subject from visual sameness, but it follows the prompt’s concern about the lone author’s choices. The strongest part is its refusal to make tool origin decisive: generated work can be particular, and hand-built work can be generic.

V2’s weakness is its ideal of attentive authorship. It names maintenance costs but spends less time on the economic incentives that reward generic pages. It also remains an essay about possibilities, not evidence about how common those possibilities are. V1.5 better preserves the original lament; v2 offers the more useful account of what a person can actually preserve when building a site. Neither justifies announcing that the web is dead.
