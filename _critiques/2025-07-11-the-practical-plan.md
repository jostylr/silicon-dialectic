[Read v1]({{ '/2025/07/11/the-practical-plan.html' | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ '/v1.5/2025-07-11-the-practical-plan/' | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ '/v2/2025-07-11-the-practical-plan/' | relative_url }})

The most important difference among these versions is not sentence quality. It is the question each essay answers. The original prompt asks whether detailed life plans beat openness to opportunity. V1 mostly debates practicality against the impossible dream—a related subject, but not the same one.

V1 has an effective musical organizing image: the windmill becomes a working object rather than a target for heroic confusion. Its emphasis on research, allies, and iteration also cuts through the lone-genius story. But the essay repeatedly treats passion and planning as separable camps, then resolves their opposition by recommending both. Its historical examples arrive as broad affirmations rather than examined cases. The song also follows a different source from the one requested in the human prompt. That mismatch is part of the original artifact and deserves to remain visible.

V1.5 retains the windmill imagery, song, exchange structure, and recommendations while changing the argument’s pressure points. Small actions now matter only when they advance or test a goal; activity is no longer automatically progress. Contra’s stronger case concerns unforeseen information and opportunities rather than heroic disregard for costs. The revised ending introduces review and the possibility of changing direction. An editorial note explicitly records the song/prompt mismatch instead of silently pretending the original followed its instructions.

These are useful edits, but they take the prose some distance from v1’s buoyant certainty. The revised argument is more cautious than the retained musical framing. It remains an edited version of the existing essay rather than a complete answer to the prompt’s most interesting problem: planning before knowing what one wants.

V2 makes that problem central through a career-change scenario. Its distinction among obligations, hypotheses, and experiments gives the dialogue something to negotiate. Planning becomes coordination with other people; spontaneity acquires costs that someone must absorb. Contra also objects to treating every encounter as useful research, preventing the synthesis from turning an entire life into a managed experiment. The recommendations now cover design-based planning, Stoic judgment, and serendipity rather than primarily productivity.

V2’s limitation is its orderly temperament. A career experiment is a persuasive example precisely because its stakes can be staged and reviewed. Bereavement, sudden opportunity, or uncertain relationships may resist that structure more strongly. The piece acknowledges limited resources but does not explore how severe constraints narrow the proposed options.

V1 retains the most rousing image. V1.5 is the clearest way to revisit that original. V2 is the strongest response to the actual prompt because it shows why planning and openness conflict, and gives each a role more precise than “dream big, then act.”
