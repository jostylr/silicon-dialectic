The central editorial repair is to preserve unavoidable harm without defining wrongdoing entirely by malice.

<!--more-->

[Read v1]({{ '/2025/07/25/life-necessitates-evil.html' | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ '/v1.5/2025-07-25-life-necessitates-evil/' | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ '/v2/2025-07-25-life-necessitates-evil/' | relative_url }})

The original has a powerful starting intuition: ordinary living participates in harms that a comfortable account of goodness can hide. Its disturbing natural examples and prayer-like song give the ethical question emotional weight. It also notices that defining every injury as evil may make morality unusable. The trouble is its proposed escape. Contra says evil concerns intention, and the synthesis narrows it to intent and delight in suffering. That leaves negligence, recklessness, and knowing indifference inadequately addressed.

The cow example illustrates the gap. Killing for nourishment differs from killing for amusement, but purpose alone does not determine whether a particular action is justified. Available alternatives and the interests of the harmed animal still matter. The original treats that distinction as if it ended the argument. Its recommendation list, meanwhile, gestures toward moral nature, war, original sin, and simple living without always explaining how those lenses differ.

V1.5 preserves the progression from harm through humility to peace. It makes the initial equation of harm and evil a definition to examine rather than a conclusion to accept. It explicitly adds foreseeable consequences, alternatives, and duties of care. It removes the misleading suggestion that benign intent settles animal ethics and gives repair a place alongside compassion. These are substantive corrections to the argument, not simply softer wording. The recommendation descriptions also identify broad theses and contested generalizations rather than treating every book as neutral authority.

V2 changes the moral setting to a community kitchen during a storm. This is a productive fresh start because doing nothing now has a visible cost. The actor cannot obtain innocence merely by withdrawing from an imperfect supply chain. The kitchen also supports a distinction between individual decisions and collective capacity: a volunteer can alter a delivery, while infrastructure requires organized effort. The original lyric's bowl, road, and hands connect its prayer to a particular scene of action.

Its strongest addition is regret without self-condemnation. A justified choice can leave a real loss, and recognizing that loss need not invalidate the choice. Its warning about turning victims into props for the actor's conscience also gives the essay a sharper ethical center than the original's search for inner peace.

The scenario has limits. Emergency food assistance makes the case for acting especially compelling. It cannot decide the ordinary consumption choices where convenience is less urgent and alternatives more contested. Neither new version offers a rule for weighing incomparable interests or deciding how demanding moral obligation should be. V1.5 is the clearest repair to the original thesis; V2 is the strongest standalone essay because it gives both action and inaction consequences. The title remains intentionally provocative, but neither text now pretends that every harmful outcome and every moral evil are identical.
