[Read v1]({{ '/2025/07/15/hobgoblins-of-consistency.html' | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ '/v1.5/2025-07-15-hobgoblins-of-consistency/' | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ '/v2/2025-07-15-hobgoblins-of-consistency/' | relative_url }})

The human prompt gives this entry unusual immediacy: it is late, a daily post is due, and the writer is deciding whether the streak deserves another hour. V1’s drum image fits that pressure well. Its best line of thought is that consistency should serve values rather than become a measure of personal worth.

Its weakness is that the philosophical and scientific references sound more decisive than they are. The claim that each post forges new neural pathways lends a biological gloss to an ordinary observation about practice. Emerson’s warning about consistency and Thoreau’s deliberate living are recruited into a modern scheduling dispute without explaining the distance between their concerns and a publishing streak. Contra’s insistence that breaking a streak is “evolution” merely reverses the automatic praise that James gives maintaining it.

V1.5 keeps the drum, the late-night situation, and the dialogue’s basic structure. It removes the neural-pathway claim, explains the narrower relevance of Emerson, and avoids presenting Thoreau as an authority on daily posting. Its sleep claim is limited and linked to NHLBI guidance. More significantly, the revision refuses automatic verdicts in either direction: keeping or breaking the streak can be sensible, depending on what happens and why. Writing each day is distinguished from publishing each day, and an earlier cutoff becomes a concrete alternative to nightly emergency decisions.

Those changes improve the essay without exhausting its central problem. A reader still needs to decide what counts as a worthwhile short post, and that judgment cannot be delegated to a rule about bedtime. The original song’s repeated beat remains more memorable than the revised prose’s careful qualifications.

V2 makes the measurement problem central. The calendar counts publication but cannot distinguish a useful paragraph from a token posted to protect the record. James gives the streak a fair defense as a way to reduce daily negotiation; Contra asks which promise the audience actually received. The distinction between a bounded month-long experiment and a sustainable publication is especially pertinent to this blog. A method can be appropriate for one and a poor design for the other.

The new essay also considers costs shifted into the following day and separates composition from a publication queue. Its advice is therefore less dependent on willpower. Yet it may overformalize a small creative habit: defining minimum quality, exceptions, and cutoffs can itself become another managerial layer around writing.

V1 remains the most compact expression of the late-night feeling. V1.5 offers the best proportion of editorial repair to original charm. V2 is the stronger analysis of the experiment’s rules, but its added framework earns its length chiefly for readers actually maintaining a recurring practice.
