[Read v1]({{ '/2025/07/17/midummer-doubts.html' | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ '/v1.5/2025-07-17-midummer-doubts/' | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ '/v2/2025-07-17-midummer-doubts/' | relative_url }})

V1 understands the mood of a summer project list turning oppressive. Its quick succession of ambitions—novel, app, basement, thesis, channel, guitar—captures how each idea can seem manageable before all of them arrive at once. The song’s cluttered planner is a good image, and the essay’s gentleness provides welcome relief from the pressure it describes.

Its explanations are less disciplined than its tone. “This isn’t failure—it’s physics” promotes a metaphor into a law. Guilt is said to mean merely that a person cares. Depression, fatigue, and burnout slide together, while Contra’s answer sometimes changes the subject from a significant project to watering plants or cleaning a drawer. The recommendation called *Summer Projects for the Whole Family* lacks an author or an identifiable edition in the entry.

V1.5 keeps the seasonal arc and the original lyrics while replacing those claims with a simpler account of mismatched commitments and capacity. AI is described as speeding first drafts without guaranteeing finished work. A small action now needs to reveal something about a meaningful project, so arbitrary busyness no longer automatically counts as progress. The dialogue asks what would count as enough and allows deliberate stopping. The unidentified recommendation is transparently replaced with Oliver Burkeman’s verifiable *Four Thousand Weeks*, rather than silently assigning the original title an invented author.

The revised piece remains short and emotionally accessible. Its restraint around mental-health language is an improvement, though it cannot tell a reader whether their particular difficulty is ordinary discouragement or something requiring a different kind of response. That is a boundary of the essay, not an omitted productivity trick.

V2 develops the strongest latent issue: individually plausible projects compete for a shared quantity of time. It distinguishes an experiment from a delivery, and a quick AI prototype from a dependable finished tool. Those distinctions stop “finish something small” from becoming permission to redefine every promise after the fact. Contra also objects to a review system becoming another project, keeping the proposed planning bounded.

The rewrite’s best concrete addition is a stopping procedure: save notes, tell affected people, release time, and put away materials. This makes abandoning a project a real decision rather than an intention followed by continued background guilt. Its song, with twelve parcels and one table, is well matched to the argument’s concern with simultaneous claims.

V2 can still sound more managerial than the situation warrants. Not every summer needs active projects classified and reviewed. It partly answers that objection by reserving time whose value is not a project’s value. V1.5 is the better brief reassurance; v2 is the better essay for someone actually facing an overflowing project list. The new version wins on practical discrimination, not because it promises more output.
