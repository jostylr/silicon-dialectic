A serious defense of child freedom needs a serious defense of teaching, and both must answer for the learner who struggles.

<!--more-->

[Read v1]({{ '/2025/07/29/learning-to-be-human.html' | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ '/v1.5/2025-07-29-learning-to-be-human/' | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ '/v2/2025-07-29-learning-to-be-human/' | relative_url }})

V1's moral energy is unmistakable: childhood is already life, and education should develop people capable of choosing, cooperating, and caring. The jailbreak framing gives its anger at coercive schooling a clear voice. It also makes conventional education almost impossible to defend fairly. Schools become factory relics, subject knowledge becomes forgettable material available on Google, and Contra is asked to endorse obedience and sorting people into usefulness. A victory over that opponent tells us little about the best case for teaching.

The evidential problems follow the rhetorical imbalance. Claims about what most adults remember and the best predictors of success appear without support. Sudbury outcomes become broad assurances, while “risk is a feature” places little weight on a child's unequal access to help. Even the song's escape to “no more rules” sits uneasily with the actual community governance the essay praises. The recommendations overwhelmingly support the same side of the argument.

V1.5 preserves the original commitment to agency but removes those shortcuts. It rejects the simple factory-origin story, distinguishes searchable information from usable background knowledge, and describes Sudbury freedom within community rules. Graduate accounts are identified as evidence of possibilities rather than causal proof. Contra now argues for access to knowledge and support rather than compliance for its own sake. Willingham's book adds the substantial opposing lens missing from the original list.

V2 begins with a child repairing a bicycle and needing to read its manual. The scenario makes self-direction and instruction mutually relevant from the start. The same project becomes harder when the child lacks a neighbor, workspace, or money. That variation reveals how an attractive freedom can depend on resources the institution must help supply. The new song's request to be taught to read is a particularly effective departure from the first song's unqualified escape.

The fresh essay's best question is what happens when something goes wrong. Can a child challenge a humiliating lesson? Can a self-directed learner request systematic help? That tests both institutional claims by their response to difficulty, rather than comparing one model's ideals with another's failures. It also avoids making predictions about AI employment the justification for treating children with dignity.

The limitation is that the bicycle is a welcoming bridge between the positions. It does not fully confront a child persistently refusing a skill adults consider essential, or a school with too few staff to offer the desired support. V2 names those tensions but proposes no operational settlement. It is the best standalone essay because its opposition is fair and its examples reveal inequality. V1.5 is the stronger preservation of the initial provocation. Neither supplies a universal school-choice recommendation; both replace certainty with questions about an actual child's opportunities and needs.
