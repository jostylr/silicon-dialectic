[Read v1]({{ '/2025/07/12/the-classics.html' | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ '/v1.5/2025-07-12-the-classics/' | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ '/v2/2025-07-12-the-classics/' | relative_url }})

This sequence turns a familiar defense of “timeless wisdom” into a more demanding question: what should a living reader actually do with an inherited book?

V1’s strongest feature is accessibility. It offers recognizable entry points—Marcus Aurelius, Plato, Shakespeare—and describes reading as joining a conversation. Its recommendations already include Confucius, so the piece is less narrowly canonical than its opening rhetoric suggests. The song supplies an easy mnemonic for the attraction of old thought. But the opposing case is overstated: Stoicism is presented as telling people to accept injustice, and historical distance becomes nearly synonymous with moral disqualification. James can defeat that objection too easily by recommending an open mind.

V1.5 preserves the invitation and the original musical material while removing those shortcuts. The influence of a particular European canon is distinguished from a claim about all human wisdom. Stoic ideas are treated as capable of misuse rather than inherently opposed to resisting injustice. Contra asks a more practical question about finite reading time and the institutions that made certain texts available and prestigious. The synthesis recommends pairing texts and attending to missing voices, giving critical reading some content beyond a posture of tolerance.

The editing also changes the force of the recommendations. *The Republic* becomes an influential argument inviting disagreement rather than “the foundation” with an uncomplicated authority. That is a small wording change with a substantial effect: a recommendation no longer smuggles in the verdict the essay is supposed to consider. Still, v1.5 carries the original’s broad survey structure and does not demonstrate its proposed paired reading.

V2 reorganizes the argument around the metaphor of inviting someone to a table. Survival establishes a book’s historical journey, not the truth of its claims. The dialogue then considers selection, translation, authorial position, and the opportunity cost of prestige. Most usefully, Contra challenges “read critically” as insufficient instruction for a beginner. The essay answers with a smaller starting procedure: choose a question, begin with a manageable work, compare contexts, and record what challenged or puzzled you.

That method is helpful, but the essay still discusses reading more than it performs it. No passage receives a close analysis, and the recommended list remains largely the familiar list it has just questioned. Adding *The Analects* cannot by itself demonstrate a changed standard of selection; v2 explicitly recognizes that problem but leaves it unresolved.

For an inviting first encounter, v1.5 may be preferable: its scale and warmth fit the blog well. V2 offers the stronger account of how authority should be tested. The gain is not that the newest version likes classics less, but that it makes the reader’s freedom and responsibility more concrete.
