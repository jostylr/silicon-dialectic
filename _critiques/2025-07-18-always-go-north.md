[Read v1]({{ '/2025/07/18/always-go-north.html' | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ '/v1.5/2025-07-18-always-go-north/' | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ '/v2/2025-07-18-always-go-north/' | relative_url }})

This is the entry where editing can most easily improve accuracy while damaging the original pleasure. V1 is a compass-shaped daydream. Pines, cold air, old towns, warm southern water, and western skies become temperaments. Its strongest feature is the ease with which the song and prose share that imaginative map.

The trouble begins when the map is treated as a description of the world. North and East acquire wisdom and roots; South and West acquire vitality and reinvention. References to ancient cities before the “New World” had a name and the idea of westward newness obscure histories already present in those places. Thoreau is invoked as a northern traveler without a useful geographical relationship to the argument, and trees are said to reach southward as if botany could settle the metaphor.

V1.5 preserves the journey, regional preferences, and original song while marking their point of view. The opening calls the maxim a traveler’s fond rule. Particular American associations replace a universal philosophy of directions. James can prefer north without claiming wisdom lives there; Contra can recommend another route without requiring the traveler to earn a return east. The revision also removes the tree claim and qualifies the uncritical description of John Muir in the recommendations.

This is careful work, but the qualifications can make a light piece feel answerable to a heavier standard than its conceit originally invited. The retained lyrics still employ the regional romance more freely than the prose does. That tension is instructive: preservation keeps the artifact recognizable, but it cannot make all its imaginative premises consistent with the new framing.

V2 finds a more flexible starting point in the distinction between preference and theorem. A traveler can select a route through taste without forcing the destination to perform that taste. The dialogue repeatedly returns to this concrete obligation: allow residents, work, change, and unexpected activity to enter the account. The NOAA source provides a modest correction about magnetic and geographic direction, and the essay explicitly avoids treating the physical fact as mystical support for its metaphor.

The original v2 lyrics are less grand than the old song but more precisely aligned with the essay: a preference for pine shade makes no claim on everyone else. Its limitation is that it remains an imagined journey. No encountered person actually disrupts the narrator’s expectations, so the recommendation to listen is easier to deliver than to demonstrate.

V1 has the strongest unguarded atmosphere. V1.5 is a conscientious repair but occasionally loses buoyancy. V2 is the best self-contained essay because it makes the act of projection its subject rather than merely correcting the projections one by one. The earlier versions still offer pleasures that the newest cannot wholly replace.
