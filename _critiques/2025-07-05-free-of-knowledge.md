The question about books becomes useful when the essay distinguishes producing an answer from knowing what it can safely support.

<!--more-->

[Read v1]({{ "/2025/07/05/free-of-knowledge.html" | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ "/v1.5/2025-07-05-free-of-knowledge/" | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ "/v2/2025-07-05-free-of-knowledge/" | relative_url }})

The original “Free of Knowledge” names a durable conflict between learning for mastery and acting through powerful tools. Its repeated song question is an effective expression of impatience: why study when a prompt appears to produce the result? Both speakers have recognizable concerns, and the entry avoids concluding that books are simply obsolete.

Its problem is the ease of the ending. “Combine” learning and action is agreeable but does not explain when either should lead. James makes broad claims about what is now most valuable “for most” people, while Contra invokes history, wisdom, and mastery at a similarly broad level. The driving analogy blurs the distinction between not needing to understand every mechanism and needing enough competence to recognize a hazard.

Version 1.5 keeps the debate and song but replaces the universal claims with differences among tasks. Reversible experiments can support learning through action; recurring responsibilities and costly failures call for a stronger foundation and independent verification. The synthesis now gives a specific check: explain an idea without the answer in front of you, then predict what a changed condition will do. The reading list adds Charles Van Doren’s coauthorship of *How to Read a Book* and explains the different uses and limits of the recommendations.

Version 2 supplies what v1 most lacked: a task in which apparent success can be misleading. The generated script combines registrations and payments, but repeated names and ambiguous records expose assumptions the novice might not know to ask about. The example shifts the issue away from loyalty to books and toward responsibility for the meaning of a result.

The new dialogue also gives the pro-action voice a better reply. The reader may learn the necessary concepts through small examples and questions, without completing a book first. Contra’s defense of sustained study then rests on coherent explanations and unfamiliar questions, not the sacredness of a medium. V2 additionally recognizes that endless reading can shelter avoidance just as prompting can shelter dependence.

V2 is the clearest and most balanced argument of the three. V1.5 is the efficient revision for readers already familiar with the issue. The remaining weakness is that the toy reconciliation problem cannot represent every technical responsibility, particularly work where failures are difficult to observe. Neither revision establishes a universal threshold of expertise. Their useful achievement is smaller: they replace “books or AI?” with an assessable question about whether the user can recognize the assumptions and limits of the work produced.
