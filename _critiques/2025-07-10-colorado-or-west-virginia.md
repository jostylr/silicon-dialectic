The relocation prompt is better served by an ordinary-week comparison than by assigning each state a national personality.

<!--more-->

[Read v1]({{ "/2025/07/10/colorado-or-west-virginia.html" | relative_url }}) · [Read v1.5]({{ "/v1.5/2025-07-10-colorado-or-west-virginia/" | relative_url }}) · [Read v2]({{ "/v2/2025-07-10-colorado-or-west-virginia/" | relative_url }})

The original “Colorado or West Virginia” is welcoming travel writing. It makes the Rockies and Appalachians sing to each other, and its alternating verses give both landscapes affection. The reader can understand why either place might attract someone without needing a winner. That generosity is a strength worth retaining.

The prompt, however, asks where to move for quality of life. V1 mostly answers where to imagine a beautiful visit. Colorado receives optimism, culture, and adventure; West Virginia receives affordability, roots, and a gentle pace. Assertions about thin crowds, lower costs, and statewide character are not grounded in particular locations. The Greenbrier appears among natural wonders without being distinguished as a resort. The recommendations lean toward sightseeing rather than relocation, and Martin Fletcher’s travel narrative is described too narrowly as an Appalachian journey.

Version 1.5 keeps the scenic rivalry but makes each claim more local and conditional. It distinguishes the resort from outdoor destinations, removes the promise of solitude, and stops treating community as an automatic property of a state. The expanded synthesis asks about actual housing, work, commutes, services, and an ordinary visit outside the most attractive season. Its added price-data source and corrected book description bring the entry closer to the question while leaving its musical frame intact.

Version 2 changes the unit of comparison. It gives the same hypothetical household two possible lives and asks what each town would make available at a full cost in money and time. That prevents Colorado from receiving an assumed good job while West Virginia receives an assumed inexpensive house, with neither assumption checked. The ordinary-week map is the essay’s strongest device because it connects beautiful destinations to the routines through which residents might reach them.

Contra also makes a useful objection to the method: a numerical score can disguise judgments about belonging, care, and identity. V2 distinguishes essential requirements from preferences and gives qualitative attachment a legitimate place. Its refusal to announce a universal winner follows from missing household details rather than mere diplomatic balance.

V2 is the stronger response to the relocation prompt. V1.5 is more pleasant as a short scenic essay. Neither provides current local quotes or a recommendation for an actual family, and v2’s additional procedure cannot remove that limit. It also loses some regional flavor by treating the places primarily as candidate lives. The tradeoff is justified here: the user’s original question concerned living somewhere, including the difficult Tuesdays that a postcard leaves outside the frame.
